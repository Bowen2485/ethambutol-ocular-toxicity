
## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 Principal findings

In 15,510 de-duplicated ethambutol cases drawn from 20.7 million FAERS cases, the ocular reporting disproportion was concentrated in the optic-nerve query: it was the highest of the eleven ocular queries tested on MedDRA's own terms, at 23.51 against 4.13 for the next, and the queries covering the ocular surface and anterior segment were at or below background. Within the query the evidence rested on optic neuropathy and optic neuritis, the terms with the largest numbers of exposed cases rather than the largest ratios. The optic-nerve-related signal was also observed in JADER, and in Canada Vigilance under a broader string-matched definition; the systems differ in reporters, in the coding of suspect drugs and in reporting culture. The non-specific term contrast was reproduced in FAERS and, for the mapped terms, JADER, whereas Canada Vigilance provided supporting evidence only for the optic-nerve signal. Median time to an optic-nerve event was 125 days, against 33 days for non-ocular events in the same case set.

### 4.2 What this adds to previous work

That ethambutol is associated with optic neuropathy is not new, and its prominent ocular signal has been reported before [14]. The contribution here is the internal contrast. Previous pharmacovigilance analyses of these drugs reported signal counts aggregated to system organ class [14] or organised the ocular phenotype by organ-system grouping [15]; in either design a specific optic-nerve injury and a general increase in ocular reporting look alike. Testing the eleven ocular queries against one another, with the optic-nerve query nominated in advance and with pre-specified non-specific terms as controls, separates those two possibilities within the same reports. The optic-nerve signal appeared in all three systems, at different evidential levels.

The design also changes what can be claimed. A signal concentrated in one anatomical category, reproduced in an independent national system, is a stronger basis for a safety hypothesis than a single elevated organ-system count, and it is the kind of pattern that can be checked in other databases. It is not evidence of incidence, and no proportion in this analysis can be set against a clinical incidence, because the denominator is reports rather than treated patients and stimulated reporting for a well-known adverse effect inflates the numerator.

### 4.3 Reporting bias, indication confounding, and what the controls do and do not show

Spontaneous reporting has no denominator, and under-reporting is substantial and non-random. Three biases are relevant, and they act differently on the main result. Notoriety bias — increased reporting of a known adverse effect — raises the absolute ocular signal but does not by itself produce a contrast between optic-nerve terms and cataract, dry eye or glaucoma, because all of them sit in the same reports. Sharing a database and an exposure definition does not, however, make every bias common to all outcomes: the clinician who suspects ethambutol toxicity may code the optic-nerve diagnosis precisely and leave a visual complaint uncoded. The comparison narrows the range of explanations but does not eliminate outcome-specific reporting: differential coding is the more serious concern, clinicians who suspect ethambutol toxicity may code optic neuropathy more precisely than they code a non-specific complaint, which would concentrate the signal in the optic-nerve terms without any difference in underlying incidence. Indication confounding acts in the opposite direction, inflating the ocular signal as a whole; the elevation of the ocular infections query is the clearest example: it may partly reflect confounding by indication and channelling, including the underlying ocular infection, rather than a direct drug effect.

What the comparison supports is narrower than a ranking of ocular toxicity. The eleven queries are not independent: preferred terms belong to more than one of them, the background frequency of each condition differs in the underlying population, and the disease mix of the reports is not the same across queries. An ordering of odds ratios is therefore a description of the reporting signal, not a test that ethambutol affects one ocular structure more than another; no contrast between queries was tested. The controls bound the conclusion in both directions. The four negative-control terms did not show positive disproportionality signals, whereas the non-specific comparators were not uniformly null: eye pain was below unity, but vision blurred showed a modest elevation (1.50, 1.28–1.76). The vision-blurred signal, and the possibility of outcome-specific recognition and coding, mean that the internal contrast cannot establish anatomical specificity or exclude reporting bias. It also cannot exclude a true effect of the drug that is small relative to background reporting. Restriction to suspect-drug reports and the period split change the size of the estimate without changing its direction. The fixed-regimen analysis — rifampicin, isoniazid and pyrazinamide with versus without ethambutol — holds the background regimen constant but is a within-class reporting contrast, not a matched cohort: treatment duration, dose, disease severity and co-medication are not held constant, and the denominator is reports rather than treated patients. It rests on a small unexposed group and its interval includes unity, so it is reported for completeness rather than as evidence of a within-class effect.

### 4.4 Limits of the transcriptomic exploration

The transcriptomic reanalysis was included because the mechanisms proposed for this toxicity make directional predictions that can be checked in human cells, not because it can resolve the mechanism. Three limits govern how it should be read. The exposure was 8 mM for 24 hours, 330 to 800 times the peak plasma concentration at standard dosage, so the experiment models an acute millimolar insult rather than the months of therapeutic exposure over which the clinical toxicity develops. The preparation is a monoculture of retinal ganglion-like cells, so contributions from microglia, Müller glia or the retinal vasculature are invisible to it, and the predictions were assembled after the data were in hand, so the analysis can show that a predicted direction was not observed but cannot confirm or refute a mechanism operating in patients. The antioxidant, autophagic and oxidative-phosphorylation programmes moved in the predicted directions; the ferroptosis account [19] predicted a fall in SLC7A11 and GPX4 and their transcripts rose, so the appropriate conclusion is that the data provide no consistent transcriptional support for the ferroptosis prediction under these conditions; they do not exclude ferroptosis, and they cannot, because transcript levels are not a flux measurement.

### 4.5 Clinical and regulatory implications

A signal of this shape identifies where to look; it is a reason for vigilance, not a basis for a screening schedule. Ethambutol optic neuropathy is managed by stopping the drug, and the damage becomes irreversible once atrophy is established, so detection before that point is what matters. In these data the reported time to onset had a median of 125 days, with 10.8% (9 of 83) of optic-nerve events reported within 30 days of starting treatment and 22.9% (19 of 83) within 60 days. That median comes from the 17-term case-level definition and the 83 reports with complete dates, not from the 432-case query, and it describes reports with complete dates rather than the incidence we would see in a monitored cohort: reports are a selected subset, the date of onset is recorded retrospectively, and no examinations that were not performed can appear in it. It is therefore consistent with a baseline examination and an early first follow-up, and with the clinical literature in which structural measures predict visual outcome better than initial acuity [10,11,12,13], but it cannot set the interval, and we do not derive one from it. Two further limits apply to these dates. Reports with an incomplete date pair are excluded, and their exclusion is unlikely to be random: a report that records both the start of therapy and the date of the event is one where the timing mattered to the reporter, which probably enriches the subset for events the reporter had already connected to the drug.

For regulators and those who build pharmacovigilance workflows, the transferable point is methodological: pre-specified MedDRA queries and ocular comparators give a structured assessment of whether disproportionality is uniform across ocular outcomes, without excluding outcome-specific recognition and coding. The design applies to any drug with a suspected site-specific toxicity and needs no data beyond what reporting systems publish.

Exposure identification carries its own limits. FAERS exposure rests on a single token in the drug-name fields, and brand and salt variants were not added to it, so a report naming only a brand product is missed; the three systems also identify exposure differently, from a drug-name substring, a suspect-drug code and an ingredient table. Two consequences follow: the ethambutol cohort is probably incomplete in a way that varies by system, and the cross-database results should be read as agreement in direction and pattern rather than as comparable odds ratios. Two designs would move the question forward, and neither can be built from spontaneous reports. A prospective cohort with protocolised baseline and interval ophthalmic assessment — optical coherence tomography and visual field testing, with the examination schedule fixed in advance rather than triggered by symptoms — would give both an incidence and the detection window. A record-linkage study of prescription data to incident optic-neuropathy diagnoses, with renal function and dose as covariates, would test the exposure–response relationship that a reporting database cannot address.

### 4.6 Conclusion

Across three spontaneous-reporting systems, ethambutol reports showed an optic-nerve-predominant pattern in FAERS and an optic-nerve-related signal in JADER and Canada Vigilance; the internal-control comparison was available in FAERS and, at term level, in JADER. These findings characterise a safety signal rather than the incidence or causal effect of ethambutol. The pattern is consistent with a site-specific toxicity and with the clinical literature on ethambutol optic neuropathy, but differential reporting and residual confounding cannot be excluded, and the transcriptomic exploration does not establish a mechanism.
